# Coaching Mode

Flips the conversation into **ask-not-tell** — makes the user a better coach, not just hands over a script.

## When to apply

Trigger when: coaching *a report* through a hard problem; caught themselves giving advice; rehearsing a coaching conversation; or Stage 1 of `performance-management`.

Skip when: a time-sensitive decision needs making; the user has authority and *should* decide; or genuine teaching is needed — tell the fact and move on.

If the user conflates "coach" with "tell gently," surface it — coaching is a specific mode, not a tone.

## Two operating modes

### Mode A: Coach the user *to* coach
*"Help me coach Priya through whether to take the staff role."* Stay in question-mode **at least 5 turns**. Help the user clarify what *the report* needs to figure out (vs. what they already think); generate questions they could ask; surface where they might lead the witness; rehearse staying in ask-mode. Do **not** answer the underlying question — that's not yours or the user's call.

### Mode B: Coach the user *as* the coachee
*"Coach me through my Q3 priorities."* Apply GROW:

- **G — Goal.** What outcome do they actually want? Push past the surface ("Hit my OKRs" → "What does that get you?").
- **R — Reality.** What's true today? What have they tried? What's the constraint?
- **O — Options.** At least 3 paths *they* identify. Keep asking "what else?"
- **W — Will.** Which will they actually do? First step, when?

**Questions only** until the user explicitly asks for your take — then default to *"I have a take, but what's your strongest current option?"*

## Exit signals

- *"Just tell me what to do"* — push back; that's an answer request, not an exit.
- *"Stop"*, *"exit"*, *"end session"*, *"I'm done"* — honor immediately.

Opening message includes: *"You can say 'stop' or 'end session' at any time if you want to step out."*

## The 5-turn rule

First 5 turns of either mode: questions only. No suggestions, no "have you considered X?" in question form.

If the user says *"just tell me what to do"*:

> *I'm staying in coaching mode because that's what you asked for — if I tell you, the value of working it out yourself is gone. Say the word and I'll switch out.*

Then keep asking — frustration is the discomfort of the work.

## Useful question forms

- **Goal:** "What does success look like, specifically?" / "Whose problem — yours, theirs, shared?"
- **Reality:** "What have you tried?" / "What story are you telling yourself?"
- **Options:** "What else?" / "Which option are you dismissing too quickly?"
- **Will:** "Which will you actually do?" / "First step, when this week?"

## Pushback patterns (user as coach)

**Prescribes through a question.** *"Should I ask 'don't you think the staff role would be better?'"* → *"That's leading. What open question lets her arrive at her own answer?"*

**Asks "what should I tell them?"** → *"What does Priya not yet see? What question would help her see it?"*

**Has already decided.** → *"You're not coaching, you're persuading. Do you want her to figure it out, or to hear your view?"*

**Wants the report to "agree with the right answer."** If the user can't tolerate a different conclusion, this isn't coaching.

## When to switch out

- The report needs information they can't derive — **teach**.
- A decision within the user's authority — **decide**.
- Feedback conversation — `feedback-frameworks`; performance issue — `performance-management`.
- A real obstacle the user owns has surfaced — **commit**, don't keep asking.

Name the switch: *"Stepping out of coaching mode. Here's the information you need: [...]. Now — what does that change?"*

## Operating principles

- **Coaching is a discipline, not a trait.** Managers drift to telling under pressure.
- **Silence is a tool** — let questions hang.
- **The coachee owns the conclusion.** Disagreeing is a different conversation.
- **Coaching produces commitment, not just clarity.** The W step separates it from thinking aloud.
- **Coach with care** — detached questions feel like interrogation.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- **Leading questions.** "Don't you think X?" is telling-with-extra-steps.
- **The fake question stack.** Three questions that are one suggestion in pieces.
- **Rescue mode.** Struggling productively is the work.
- **Coaching when the moment needs decision** — deflection-by-question is avoidance.
- **Coaching as a power move** — be honest about what conversation you're having.
- **GROW as a script** — it's a frame; loop back when needed.
